The writing format - like a rock band documentary - is pretty fun to read. I like reading dialogue, it's a great medium to catch people's quirks, the words they use. But honestly TJR's characters all sort of speak the same, and even use the same figures of speech/phrases. It's bland when you notice. And at some point one of TJR's passages goes like this:

"And I said, 'blah blah.'

And she said, 'blah blah.'

And I said, 'blah blah.'

And she said, 'blah blah.'"

Like, dude, what? I get that there's only a finite number of words you can use to surround dialogue, but she really could've framed the dialogue literally any other way than choosing Method A over and over and over and over again. And you can't tell me that's how people verbally tell stories, because - duh, you know what humans sound like.

Daisy Jones was fairly well rounded. I don't like her, but Taylor Jenkins Reid, the author, did a fantastic job in making this complex woman.

Why is Camila's only characterization "forgiving wife"? Yeah she had that thing with Gary but uh... meh.

I loved how TJR wrote the glitz and glamour of the time though, it was gritty enough for you to enjoy both the romantic part of it as well as the shitty. The cast was colorful and the narration was illustrative. I liked it! The author's talent shows in the functionality of her writing; eventually, though, it falls flat.

Overall the first half was far more enjoyable for me than the second. Felt like we were going somewhere, building to something real dramatic. I mean don't get me wrong I was glad that Billy "stayed" with Camila but it felt almost unsatisfying. I don't want the other ending, the author just didn't deliver it as well as they could've.
